# Device Hygiene

Cybersecurity isn't just about what you do online; it's also about how you maintain your physical devices and the networks they connect to.

## Software Updates
Software updates do more than add new features—they patch security vulnerabilities.
- **Enable Automatic Updates:** Whenever possible, set your Operating System, web browser, and applications to update automatically.
- **Don't Delay:** When an update requires a restart, do it promptly. Attackers reverse-engineer patches to attack people who haven't updated yet.

## Public Wi-Fi Risks
Public Wi-Fi networks (at coffee shops, airports, or hotels) are fundamentally insecure. You don't know who controls the network or who else is connected to it.
- Avoid accessing sensitive accounts (like banking) on public Wi-Fi.
- **Use a VPN (Virtual Private Network):** A VPN creates an encrypted tunnel between your device and the internet, protecting your traffic from local snooping.
- Turn off automatic connection to open Wi-Fi networks.

## The Importance of Backups
There are two types of computer users: those who have lost data, and those who will. Between ransomware, hardware failure, and physical theft, data loss is inevitable.
- **The 3-2-1 Rule:** Keep **3** copies of your data, on **2** different types of media, with **1** copy stored off-site (like in the cloud).
- **Test Your Backups:** A backup is only useful if you can successfully restore it.

## Physical Security
If an attacker gains physical access to your device, it's game over.
- Always lock your screen when stepping away from your computer.
- Enable full-disk encryption (BitLocker on Windows, FileVault on macOS) so that if your laptop is stolen, your data cannot be read.
