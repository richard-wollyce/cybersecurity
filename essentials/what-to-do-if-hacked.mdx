# What To Do If You Get Hacked

Even with the best precautions, incidents happen. Knowing how to react quickly can minimize the damage and help you regain control.

## 1. Disconnect and Isolate
If you suspect your computer or phone is infected with malware or ransomware:
- **Disconnect from the network immediately.** Turn off Wi-Fi, unplug the Ethernet cable, and put the device in Airplane mode.
- Do not plug in any external USB drives, as the malware might infect them.

## 2. Change Passwords (From a Safe Device)
If an account has been compromised, or if your password was stolen:
- Use a **different, clean device** to change your passwords.
- Start with your most critical accounts: Email, Banking, and Password Managers.
- If you used the stolen password anywhere else, change it there too.
- Immediately enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) on the compromised accounts.

## 3. Monitor Your Finances
If financial information or identity details were exposed:
- Check your bank and credit card statements for unauthorized transactions.
- Contact your bank to cancel compromised cards.
- Consider placing a "fraud alert" or a "credit freeze" with credit reporting bureaus to prevent attackers from opening loans in your name.

## 4. Warn Your Contacts
Attackers often use compromised email or social media accounts to send phishing links to the victim's friends and family.
- Let your contacts know that your account was compromised and they should ignore recent messages from you.

## 5. Scan and Rebuild
If a device was compromised:
- Run a full scan using reputable antivirus software.
- In severe cases (like persistent malware or ransomware), the only safe option is to completely wipe the device (factory reset) and reinstall the operating system.
- Restore your files from a clean, pre-infection backup.
