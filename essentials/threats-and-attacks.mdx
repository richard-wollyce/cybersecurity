# Common Digital Threats

Understanding how attackers operate is the first step in defending against them. Here are the most common threats you will encounter.

## Phishing and Social Engineering

**Phishing** is when an attacker sends a fraudulent message designed to trick a person into revealing sensitive information or deploying malicious software. 
- **How it works**: Attackers often impersonate banks, delivery services, or colleagues.
- **Red Flags**: Urgent language, unexpected attachments, mismatched sender email addresses, and suspicious links.

**Social Engineering** is the broader concept of manipulating people into breaking normal security procedures. Attackers exploit human psychology (fear, curiosity, or desire to help) rather than technical vulnerabilities.

## Malware and Ransomware

**Malware** (Malicious Software) is any software intentionally designed to cause damage to a computer, server, client, or computer network.
- **Viruses & Trojans**: Software that disguises itself as legitimate but steals data or provides backdoor access.
- **Ransomware**: A specific type of malware that encrypts a victim's files. The attacker then demands a ransom (usually in cryptocurrency) to restore access.

## Credential Theft

Attackers actively try to steal usernames and passwords. They do this through:
- **Data Breaches**: Stealing large databases of passwords from compromised websites.
- **Keyloggers**: Malware that records everything you type.
- **Credential Stuffing**: Taking passwords leaked from one site and automatically trying them on hundreds of other sites.

## How to Protect Yourself
- Always verify the sender's identity before clicking links.
- Keep your software updated to patch vulnerabilities that malware exploits.
- Never reuse passwords across multiple sites.
